Era Classifier: Can a Song’s Lyrics Place It in Its Decade?

This page trains and evaluates supervised classifiers that predict a song’s decade of origin from its lyrical embedding. All heavy computation is cached to disk on first run — subsequent renders load saved artifacts.

3. Dimensionality Reduction via PCA

A single StandardScaler is fit on the training set only to ensure no data leakage. We then apply PCA to the scaled features, retaining 95% of variance. This helps reduce the 389-dimensional feature space to a more manageable number of principal components while preserving most of the information.

229 PCs retained, explaining 95.0% of variance.

Cumulative explained variance of the top 100 principal components. The embedding space is moderately dense. 100 components are required to explain roughly 90% of variance.

4. Baseline: Logistic Regression

Multinomial logistic regression is run in two configurations: on 100 PCA components and on the full 389-dimensional scaled feature space to isolate the effect of dimensionality reduction independently of the model class.

LR (PCA) Val F1: 0.448  Test F1: 0.510
LR (Full) Val F1: 0.449  Test F1: 0.504
LR (PCA) Val Acc: 0.458  Test Acc: 0.530
LR (Full) Val Acc: 0.457  Test Acc: 0.524

Surprisingly, the PCA-reduced model slightly outperforms the full model in terms of both F1-score and accuracy, suggesting that dimensionality reduction may help mitigate noise and overfitting in this case. However, the differences are small, and even though it performs better than random chance (0.20), there is still significant room for improvement.

5. XGBoost Classifier (PCA Features)

We also conducted a 5-fold cross-validated grid search over key hyperparameters. The best configuration is then evaluated on the validation set. Accuracy is used as the grid search metric since the dataset is balanced.

Since XGBoost has built-in feature selection, it may perform well even without PCA. However, we start with PCA features to maintain a consistent comparison with logistic regression and to reduce computational load during hyperparameter tuning.

Best params: {'colsample_bytree': 0.8, 'learning_rate': 0.05, 'max_depth': 6, 'min_child_weight': 1, 'n_estimators': 600, 'subsample': 0.8}
Best CV F1: 0.500
XGB-PCA Val macro-F1: 0.432, Test macro-F1: 0.517
XGB-PCA Val Acc: 0.444, Test Acc: 0.540

Even though the XGBoost model with PCA features achieves a test F1-score of 0.517, slightly outperforming logistic regression’s 0.510, the difference is small. This suggests that while the non-linear modeling capacity of XGBoost may be capturing more complex relationships in the data, the pattern is not easy to discern. The validation F1 is lower than logistic regression, indicating potential overfitting to the training set.

6. XGBoost Classifier (Full Embeddings, No PCA)

XGBoost’s built-in feature selection may obviate the need for explicit PCA. This model trains on the full 389-feature space using the same best hyperparameters found above.

XGB-Full Val macro-F1: 0.478, Test macro-F1: 0.538
XGB-Full Val Acc: 0.485, Test Acc: 0.561

XGB-Full Val macro-F1: 0.478, Test macro-F1: 0.538 XGB-Full Val Acc: 0.485, Test Acc: 0.561

The full XGBoost model achieves the best performance across all configurations. The full model’s test F1 of 0.538 is a meaningful boost over the PCA version’s 0.517, and the validation F1 also improves from 0.432 to 0.478.

Hence this model may benefit from having access to the full feature space and can effectively select the most informative features. The improvement over the PCA-reduced version indicates that some of the variance captured by the discarded principal components may contain useful signal for decade classification.

8. SHAP Feature Importance

SHAP values are computed on the XGBoost (Full) model, so named features especially the engineered lyrical metrics and is_english will appear directly rather than opaque principal components. 500 test-set tracks are sampled for efficiency and cached to disk.

Mean absolute SHAP value for each engineered feature, broken down by decade class. Features with high SHAP for a specific decade are the most discriminative signals for that era. ‘is_english’ is consistently important for the 2020s, confirming that the model uses language as a decade signal.

One striking pattern is the spike in repetition ratio during the 2010s, suggesting that highly repetitive song structures became especially influential in that era. This possibly reflects trends like hook-driven pop or streaming-optimized music. In contrast, lyric word count and rhyme density show more moderate and somewhat stable contributions, though there’s a hint of an inverse relationship in some decades (e.g., the 1990s-2010s), where denser rhymes may come with shorter lyrics.

Sentiment appears to have relatively low importance overall, with small fluctuations rather than a clear trend. The is_english feature remains minor, but its slight increase in the 2020s could suggest growing linguistic diversity in popular music. Overall, the chart highlights how structural elements like repetition have become more dominant over time, while traditional lyrical complexity plays a more nuanced role.

Top 20 features overall by mean absolute SHAP value (XGBoost Full). Engineered features are highlighted in orange; embedding dimensions are in blue. The presence of interpretable features near the top validates the feature engineering effort.

We can see that engineered features like repetition_ratio, lyric_word_count, and rhyme_density are among the top overall features, confirming that the model is leveraging these interpretable lyrical properties much more than any single embedding dimension. is_english is not in the top 20 overall, which is surprising given its contextual importance in the modern era.

What Do the Top PCs Encode? Correlation with Engineered Features

For the PCA model, we can partially interpret which lyrical properties the most important principal components capture by correlating PC scores against the interpretable engineered features.

Pearson correlation between each of the top 10 PCA components and the 5 engineered features, computed on the test set. Dark cells indicate that a PC has a meaningful linear relationship with a named feature, providing a bridge between the opaque PCA space and interpretable lyrical properties.

PC1 dominates the correlation structure, showing strong positive associations with is_english (r = 0.87) and rhyme_density (r = 0.67), suggesting it primarily captures a dimension related to English-language lyrical structure. sentiment also loads moderately on PC1 (r = 0.35) and PC2 (r = 0.25). The remaining components show weak correlations with all engineered features, indicating they capture semantic variation in the embedding space that is not directly expressed by the five hand-crafted metrics. Overall, the engineered features explain only a narrow slice of the PCA space, which is expected given that 384 of the 389 input dimensions are dense embedding coordinates.

9. UMAP Projection

A 2-dimensional UMAP projection of the test-set embeddings visualizes how songs cluster in the lyrical space, and where the model’s predictions align or diverge from ground truth.

Test set: 1,492 tracks | Time travelers: 63 (4.2%)

Recall that we define time travelers as songs from the 2010s or 2020s that are misclassified as 1980s or 1990s. Using our best model so far (XGB Full), in 1492 tracks in the test set, the model detects 63 time travalers, accounting for 4.2% of the test set. This is a substantial number of songs that the model believes have a vintage lyrical style despite being released in the modern era, providing a meaningful sample for analyzing the nostalgia sells hypothesis.

UMAP projection of the test set colored by true decade.

Test-set UMAP colored by classification outcome. Red points are misclassified; grey are correct. Time travelers (2010s/2020s predicted as 1980s/1990s) are highlighted in gold.

10. The Nostalgia Sells Hypothesis

The secondary hypothesis predicts that modern songs (2010s/2020s) whose lyrics are misclassified as vintage (1980s/1990s) will have higher listener engagement than correctly classified modern songs, supporting the “nostalgia sells” theory.

Modern tracks with pageviews: 3,244
  Time Travelers:  116
  Correctly classified: 3,128

  Correct: median pageviews = 90,130  mean = 435,056
  Time Travelers: median pageviews = 65,422  mean = 364,626

Across the full dataset of modern tracks (2010s/2020s), the model identifies 116 time travelers out of 3,244 songs with pageview data.

The median pageviews for time travelers is 65422 compared to 90130 for correctly classified modern songs. The mean pageviews are 364626 for time travelers and 435056 for correctly classified songs. Both median and mean pageviews are actually lower for the time travelers, providing no support for the nostalgia sells hypothesis in this sample. However, it could also be due to the long tail of pageviews and the fact that only a small fraction of modern songs are classified as vintage, which may limit the statistical power of this analysis.
