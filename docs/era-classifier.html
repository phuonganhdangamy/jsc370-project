Era Classifier: Can a Song’s Lyrics Place It in Its Decade?

This page trains and evaluates supervised classifiers that predict a song’s decade of origin from its lyrical embedding. All heavy computation is cached to disk on first run — subsequent renders load saved artifacts.

1. Feature Preparation

Master dataset: 9,176 tracks | {'1980s': 1841, '1990s': 1837, '2000s': 1815, '2010s': 1760, '2020s': 1923}

2. Artist-Stratified 70/15/15 Split

Each artist’s entire catalogue is assigned to a single split, preventing the model from learning artist-specific style rather than era characteristics.

3. Dimensionality Reduction via PCA

A single StandardScaler is fit on the training set only (no data leakage), then 100 principal components are extracted. This reduces the 389-dimensional feature space (384 embedding dims + 5 engineered features) while retaining the majority of variance.

100 PCs explain 73.0% of variance. 101 PCs needed for 95%.

Cumulative explained variance of the top 100 principal components. The embedding space is moderately dense. 100 components are required to explain roughly 90% of variance.

8. SHAP Feature Importance

SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values quantify each feature’s contribution to the model’s predictions. For the PCA-based XGBoost, the 100 principal components are ranked by mean absolute SHAP value averaged across all five decade classes.

Top 20 principal components by mean absolute SHAP value, averaged across all five decade classes. Higher values indicate that a PC is more influential in the model’s era predictions.

Mean absolute SHAP value per PC broken down by decade class. Distinct PCs drive classification of different eras.

9. UMAP Projection

A 2-dimensional UMAP projection of the test-set embeddings visualizes how songs cluster in the lyrical space, and where the model’s predictions align or diverge from ground truth.

Test set: 1,492 tracks | Time travelers: 79 (5.3%)

UMAP projection of the test set colored by true decade. Clusters are visible but overlapping, reflecting that lyrics alone do not perfectly separate eras.

Test-set UMAP colored by classification outcome. Red points are misclassified; grey are correct. Time travelers (2010s/2020s predicted as 1980s/1990s) are highlighted in gold.

10. The Nostalgia Sells Hypothesis

The secondary hypothesis predicts that modern songs (2010s/2020s) whose lyrics are misclassified as vintage (1980s/1990s) will have higher listener engagement than correctly classified modern songs, supporting the “nostalgia sells” theory.
